Problem

A Line's form is four fields that agree only by convention, and each change is a body removal. Everything hosted on the Line hears it as gone. Grounding is walked twice, once outward in groundTouched and once inward in collapse, and Collapse runs only where a caller remembers it.

Solution

Put a Line's form, its grounding walk and its changes in one module behind Strokes. A change of form keeps the Line's Parties, as a snapshot rebuild already does, and runs quietly. Collapse follows every fixed removal, and a split keeps its Stroke, so the Game's splits map goes.

Wins
  • Bonds and Patches survive form changes
  • locality: grounding, one walk
  • Collapse can't be forgotten
  • Game loses splits, 9 sites
  • preview and outcome agree
  • tests: grounding and collapse unchanged
Confirmed with a scratch run through SandboxWorld. A green box glued under a grey Line propped on a post, as in green.test.ts's hangUnderLine, has 1 bond. Erasing the post's foot makes the Line collapse, and the box has 0 bonds before any step. A green box glued under a Frozen Line that a post then grounds goes from 1 bond to 0 the same way. Patches use the same gone path. No test in collapse.test.ts or grounding.test.ts mentions bonds or Patches. CONTEXT.md says a Bond "lasts until either side breaks, is undone or is removed by a cap", and that the green Object "never sticks again". Neither Collapse nor grounding is one of those. Only strokes.ts:568 mentions it, for grounding only.
For the grilling: should a green Object glued to a collapsing run fall with it, still glued, and should a Patch ride along? Or is letting go intended, in which case the glossary's Bond needs a fourth way to end? And should a Line keep its Stroke id when it is split?

Problem

"An Enemy is one upright box" is assumed in about 20 places across 5 files. The Siege Walker has no seam to plug into. The GDD beats it by tripping it, which an upright body can't do. A Blast would also hurt it once per leg.

Solution

Put the Enemy's body behind a small interface the Enemy rules ask: where are its feet, what is ahead and touching, its outline, its Parties. Today's box is one adapter, and the Siege Walker is the second. It is built on the one-body-many-Parties path loose Lines already use.

Wins
  • two adapters: a real seam
  • leverage: rules unchanged for the boss
  • damage once per Enemy
  • reuses PartEntry, removePart
  • tests: one fake body
Pairs with ADR 0010, 0011 and 0013 (capped-force walking and climbing). It keeps them as they are; only where "feet" and "ahead" come from changes. Best taken into the milestone 6 spec, not before it.

Problem

An Enemy type is a closed union that every table repeats. A Wave must count every type, and the order of ENEMY_TYPES decides the order of arrival, so a boss always comes last.

Solution

Put one catalogue entry per type, holding everything that varies by type. A Wave names only what it sends, and when, if it needs to.
