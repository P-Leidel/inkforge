Problem

The Tank rules are private helpers across 11 of the Game's methods, and the gauges and F1 each read the Tanks their own way. They can only be tested through a Game over Box2D. The gauge maths that the readable-cost check (#65) relies on has no test at all. Milestone 4 rewrites every one of these rules for Locked Ink and Wave Ink.

Solution

One module holds each Colour's Ink and maximum. It owns spending, refunds, whether a price can be paid, and emptying down to a lowered maximum. Milestone 4 adds locking at the Wave and taking a drop. Its state is its snapshot, and the gauges and F1 draw from one reading of it. The Game keeps pricing and what each Stroke, Piece and Fill paid.

Wins
  • locality: Tank rules in one module
  • ADR 0005 lands in one place
  • Tanks tested with plain numbers
  • gauges and F1 share one reading
  • Game keeps pricing and undo
One small inconsistency this would settle: the Game's start-over branch (game.ts:502) forgets what was paid but leaves the Tanks as they are, while clear() fills them. Only a clear or R made below the Game reaches that branch.

Problem

The world makes nine calls in an order that must never change, and carries the list of what broke past sticking and landing by hand. The reason for the order is tested nowhere: a green Object sticks to a contact that breaks in the same step, and a Patch laid on something broken goes with it. The replay tests catch an order that changes, not one that is wrong. Milestone 4 adds about five phases, and Ink drops drawn from random make their slot part of replay order.

Solution

The Material rules take the step's report in one call and run every consequence in their own order. They reach the kinds through the Arena port they already have. Blast spreading and used-up Patches move behind the rules. The world keeps physics, the snapshot and its loops over every kind.

Wins
  • interface: nine calls become one
  • order tested with existing fakes
  • M4 phases land in one module
  • replay order lives in one place
After #50 this was Speculative, to be revisited once the milestone 4 spec named its enemy phases. That spec comes next, so settle this in it, before any enemy phase is added. The cost is that the Arena port grows by about four methods: the Objects that stick, the gluers, Blast spreading and used-up Patches.

Problem

A thing's numbers are keyed on a Colour and a role in two modules, the Material rules and Arena bodies. Rubble, Droplets and Patches borrow an Outline's or a Line's surface, and the role 'line' also stands for "fixed". What breaking lets out is a Strokes type with three nullable fields, and Strokes and the rules import each other. One module answers what a thing's numbers are for every kind of thing, enemies included: surface, damage threshold, durability, impact limit, and whether it is fixed. F2 edits are re-applied from it. What breaking lets out becomes one variant per thing, owned by the rules.

Problem

Three questions are asked about the Stroke being drawn: whether it overlaps (for closing Strokes only), what it costs, and, in the renderer, whether it closes. The cost estimate carries its own copy of the Ink formula in the Game, against ADR 0009 and the Game's own doc comment. Drawing input keeps two throttles and five cache fields to serve them. Solution

One Game query takes the samples and a Colour. It answers whether they would make a Line or an Object, whether they would be refused and why, and what they would cost. Problem

The physics interface has one add method for each kind that milestones 1 and 2 needed. Whether a kind reports hits, can be Frozen or moves is decided by flags keyed on the kind, in five places in the adapter. A walker has to stay upright and be driven, and nothing in the interface or the engine declarations allows that. Touching pairs carry no direction, so the rules can't tell standing on a Line from pressing against it. Wall pressing and walking up 45° slopes both need that difference.

Solution

Callers describe a body once: its shapes and what it does. The adapter already builds every body from such a description internally. A walker then becomes a new description, instead of a new add method and five flags. Touching pairs come with their direction, so the Contact ledger and the Material rules decide floor, wall and pressing in one place.

Wins
  • enemies need no new add method
  • kinds stop leaking into the adapter
  • four add methods become one
  • floor or wall decided once
The Rubble, Spill and Blast commits each touched 14–20 source files, every time including physics-world.ts, the adapter and the engine declarations. Keep the one adapter: the real engine is fast in tests, so a fake would only test less. SandboxWorldOptions.createPhysics (sandbox-world.ts:139) has no callers and can go.

Problem

One kind's drawing is spread over up to nine places in the World renderer: fields, the constructor, what it holds, the draw order, making, freeing, following the list, dropping everything, and its draw method. The debug overlay and the verdict each list every view kind by hand. Milestone 4 adds three enemy types and the Ink Core to all of these.

Solution

Each kind gets one drawing module that makes, bakes again, frees and draws its own things from the list of what happened. The renderer reads the list, hands each entry to its kind and draws the kinds in order, the way the Sandbox world runs its kinds. The overlay and the verdict walk world.contents.

Wins
  • locality: a kind's look in one module
  • an enemy's drawing lands in one file

  • render budget test stays as it is
Worth exploring rather than Strong: today's kinds are finished, and the render budget test guards the result. This pays off when enemies are drawn, so do it just before, not now.

Problem

A new Stroke asks three questions about place, and only one goes to the Arena query. Where a Line is cut comes from a raw list of Terrain polygons. What a squeezed Object must end clear of is built by Strokes scanning every Line and every Object, and it leaves Rubble out without saying why. Solution

Line cutting and the Squeeze both ask the Arena query. Anything Arena bodies holds, enemies and the Ink Core included, then takes part as soon as it is a body. The test-only path goes, and its tests move to the query's interface.

Wins
  • enemies take part as bodies
  • Strokes stops scanning itself
  • tests run the path the game runs
  • Rubble in or out, decided once
Squeeze results are part of replay order. As with #36, pin the Squeeze's current outcomes first; shortestWayOut must see the same obstacles in the same order.
