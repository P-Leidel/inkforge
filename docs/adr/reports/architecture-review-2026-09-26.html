Problem

The spec gives the Material rules one module (m2-colours.md:141), but they are spread over MaterialRules, Glue, Sticking, five kinds and the Sandbox world. Since #27 the world has regrown from 428 to 646 lines, and it now holds Blast arrival, glue wear and Fill release where only whole-world tests on Box2D reach them.

Solution

One Material rules module takes each step's hits, new contacts, touching glue and Blast arrivals, and decides every consequence: damage from any cause, what breaks, what a break releases, what a Blast pushes. The world keeps the step order and hands those decisions to the kinds and the physics module.

Wins
  • locality: damage decided in one place
  • #20's Piece Blast: one rule
  • rules tested without the engine
  • world shrinks to the step order
  • M4 enemy damage plugs in
  • spec and code agree
Damage is added in three places and "broken" is decided three times (material-rules.ts 92, 103 · sandbox-world.ts 624–625). 11 of the 17 test files in src/sandbox build the whole world on Box2D. Sticking, blastReached and the release order have no test of their own, and glue.test.ts copies wearGluer to test glue.

Problem

Box2D can't unfreeze a fixed body, so Release, a wake and the end of a slide rebuild it, and every attachment is carried over by hand. Bonds and Patch capsules each took about eight edits across the adapter. bullet never reached rebuild, so a rebuilt circle would lose continuous collision. That is latent today, since only Objects are rebuilt.

Solution

The adapter keeps one complete description of each body: its shapes and added capsules with their surfaces, density, damping, group, bullet, wake flag and bonds. Creating, rebuilding, setSurface and setMass all apply that description.

Wins
  • locality: one record per body
  • drift like bullet can't recur
  • one rebuild test, every attachment
  • enemy body options land once
  • PhysicsWorld interface unchanged
  • a Rapier adapter reads one record

Problem

A Frozen Object waits for "a hard hit, a strong enough Blast or the player's Release" (CONTEXT.md). The first two are measured differently in two homes. Hits are judged inside the Box2D adapter, by a free-collision impulse that is then replayed. Blasts are judged in the Sandbox world, by a capped push over mass. Droplets opt out through an adapter flag.

Solution

For each hit on a Frozen Object, the adapter reports the impulse a free collision would give, and it replays the wakes it is handed. One engine-neutral wake rule decides for hits and Blasts, and says which Parties never wake anything.

Wins
  • locality: one wake rule
  • hits and Blasts agree by construction
  • adapter keeps no game rule
  • ADR 0001's Rapier swap cheaper
  • wakeSpeed read live, not copied
Found on the way (plausible, not reproduced): after a wake, the adapter rewrites the impulse of the first hit between the two bodies (982–987), not the shape pair that woke the Object. A multi-part hit keeps a wall-sized impulse on its other pairs, and the damage rule takes the strongest. The replay must also stay inside the same step, because exact replays depend on that order. Since reproduced and fixed (10a0616): every hit between a woken Object and its hitter now reports its own free-collision impulse.

Problem

Every read of world.objects rebuilds each view with up to ten engine calls per Object, and the renderer, F1 and the stress tests each read every frame. The step reports nothing, so tests and #21 poll contents and count new ids. Visual-only Debris, fading ghosts and Patch puffs run inside the headless world.

Solution

Each step updates one read model from the bodies that moved, plus a list of what happened: broke, exploded, spilled, landed, stuck, capped. The renderer, F1, tests and the verdict read those. Debris moves to the renderer and starts from that list.

Wins
  • cost follows what moved
  • #21 counts events, not ids
  • 8 pass-through getters go
  • Kind loses dropVisuals
  • headless world: simulation only
  • M4's ink drops hook on kills
Measure first: #21's Demolition step times show whether the read model is needed before enemies arrive. The list of what happened pays off either way.

Problem

Four questions about place are answered four ways: may this Object go here, which Object is under the pointer, which Objects does a new Line cross, and what does a Blast reach. Three of them move every body into world space and scan them all. The Kind interface carries solids and surfaceOf only to feed them, and only 2 of the 6 kinds answer.

Solution

One module keeps every Party's shapes in world space, current each step, with the engine's broadphase as its index. It answers point, overlap, crossing, radius and surface questions for every caller, M3's overlap charging included.

Wins
  • leverage: one query, five callers
  • Kind drops solids and surfaceOf
  • M3 overlap charging is a query
  • cost follows the neighbourhood
  • geometry tested at one interface

Problem

Every Line, Object, piece of Rubble and now every Patch is its own Graphics made of many draw commands, and Droplets and Blasts are redrawn every frame. A Colour's look is spread over two switches with no default, two crack checks and CSS whose black differs from INK_HUES. The hues of Bonds (green) and Blasts (red) are hard-coded.

Solution

An ink look module draws each look once into a cached texture, which Rubble, Droplets and Patches share as sprites. Each Colour's look is one entry.

Wins
  • draw cost flat per thing
  • sixth Colour look: one entry
  • sprites batch; Graphics don't
  • no Colour names standing in for rules
Check first: whether Phaser 4 replays a Graphics' command list every frame, and #22's frame times.

Also noticed, not candidates

  • sandbox-world.ts 433–434 still says red Fills release nothing.
