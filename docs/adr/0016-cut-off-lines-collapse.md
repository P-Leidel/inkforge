# Cut-off Lines collapse when a Piece goes

Status: Accepted. Amends the last paragraph of [ADR 0014](0014-ungrounded-lines-start-frozen.md). Its fourth paragraph is superseded by [ADR 0017](0017-lines-keep-their-stroke-when-they-change-form.md): a Line cut in two stays one Line, its cut-off stretches falling as Runs of it.

ADR 0014 left a Grounded Line fixed whatever broke, so a wall whose foot a Heavy wore through, or a platform a Blast cut off its post, went on hanging in mid-air. That looks like a bug, and it takes away the most satisfying thing a wall can do: come down. So grounding is checked again whenever a fixed Piece goes: when it breaks, when the Eraser removes it, and when undo takes back a Grounded Line. Erasing is not breaking, but it changes what is connected just the same.

Grounding is worked out on **connected runs of Pieces**, not on whole Lines: two fixed Pieces are connected when they touch within the ground tolerance, as grounding itself has it, whether they are neighbours along one Line or of two Lines. A run that still reaches the Terrain or the Ink Core stays fixed; one that doesn't is cut off and **falls at once**. It is not Frozen: a Frozen ruin floating where the wall stood would look like a bug too. If a Piece in the middle of a Line breaks, the side still connected stays and the other side falls.

Each cut-off run falls as **its own rigid body**, still with no joints ([ADR 0003](0003-no-joints-in-mvp.md)): of the Pieces cut off together, each Line's Pieces that touch each other are one body, so several Lines cut off together fall as several bodies. Once fallen, a run is a fallen Line: loose, weighing its ink and dealing impact damage like any other, never Grounded again, and Frozen again in the Aftermath when at rest. Its Pieces keep their places along the Line and their damage.

A Line cut in two keeps its id for what still stands, or for its first run if nothing does; each other run falls as a Line of its own, with a new id, and says so (`split`) before its Pieces come back under that id. The Game counts a split-off Line as part of the Stroke it was drawn as: its Pieces keep what they paid, the Eraser refunds them, and one undo takes back the Stroke and every run of it. R brings back the Arena as it was when physics started, uncollapsed.

The check is local: it starts from the fixed Pieces that touched the one that went, follows the fixed Pieces they touch through the Arena query's broadphase, and stops as soon as a run is found to reach the ground. Its cost grows with the Pieces connected to the break, not with the Arena. It runs once a step, after the Material rules, so every Piece that broke in that step has gone before anything falls, and once an Eraser stroke is done. Measured headless, a break far from the only post of a 420-Piece lattice (more Line than every Ink Tank holds together) costs about 5 ms to find it still stands; the stress tests show no change in step time.

The wobbly structures ADR 0002 warned about show up where cut-off Lines were drawn onto each other: a shelf drawn from a post's centre line, or uprights crossing shelves, overlap once they are separate bodies, and the engine pushes them apart as they fall. A post with a few shelves settles in four to five seconds; a dense lattice of crossing Lines tumbles for longer. Lines that touch are already drawn overlapping like this, so we accept it for now; if playtests find heaps that won't settle, the fix belongs with how loose Lines that overlap meet, not with collapse.
